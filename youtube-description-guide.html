How to Write a YouTube Description That Helps Viewers and Search

Published November 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Most creators either leave the description empty or paste a wall of keywords. Both waste a useful space. A good description tells viewers and YouTube what the video is about, gives people a reason to keep watching and points them to your next step.

What a description does

A description has three jobs. First, it helps YouTube understand the topic of your video, along with the title, the spoken words and the visuals. Second, it helps viewers decide whether to watch, since the first lines appear in search results and under the video. Third, it gives you room for chapters, links and credits. A description can be up to 5,000 characters, but you almost never need all of that.

Write the first two lines for people

Only the first part of your description is visible before someone taps "more". In search results, roughly the first 100 to 150 characters may appear. Treat these lines like a second title: say what the video shows and who it helps, and use the natural phrase a viewer would search for.

Weak: Hey guys, welcome back to my channel. Today we are doing something fun. Please like and subscribe.

Better: Learn how to make creamy garlic pasta in 15 minutes with only six ingredients. Perfect for busy weeknights, with no special equipment needed.

The better version names the dish, the time, the benefit and the audience. It reads naturally and contains the words a person would search for.

Add a short summary

After the opening, write two to four sentences about what happens in the video. Mention the main topics in plain language. This helps viewers who scan, and it gives YouTube more context. Do not repeat the same phrase ten times. Say it once or twice and then describe the topic in different words.

Add chapters

Chapters split your video into labeled sections and let viewers jump to the part they need. To create them, add a list of timestamps in the description:

  • The first timestamp must be 0:00.
  • You need at least three timestamps.
  • Each chapter must be at least 10 seconds long.

0:00 What we are making
0:45 Ingredients
2:10 Cooking the sauce
5:30 Plating and tips

Chapters are helpful for longer videos, tutorials and interviews. They can also improve the experience in search, because YouTube may show key moments from your video.

Links and calls to action

Put the most important link near the top, right after the first lines, so it is visible. Good things to link: a related video, a playlist, your website or your social profile. Keep it to a small number of links and say what each one is. If you use affiliate links or you were paid or given a product, you must disclose it clearly, both in the video and in the description, and use YouTube's paid promotion setting when it applies.

Hashtags

You can add hashtags in the description. YouTube shows up to three above your title. If you use more than 15, YouTube ignores all of them, so keep it to three to five relevant ones at the end of the description. Read our guide on how many hashtags to use and where to put them for more detail.

A simple template

[Line 1: what the video shows and who it helps, with your main phrase]

[Line 2: the main benefit or result]

[2 to 4 sentences summarizing the video]

Watch next: [link to related video or playlist]

Chapters:
0:00 ...
1:00 ...
2:30 ...

Credits, music and gear (if any)

#MainTopic #SecondTopic

You can generate ideas for the main phrase and hashtags from your own video with the Trenndly Analyze tool. Then edit them so they sound like you.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Keyword stuffing: long lists of unrelated search terms look spammy and can violate YouTube's rules on misleading metadata.
  • Copying the same description on every video: each video should have its own summary.
  • Hiding the point: burying the useful information under long social media lists.
  • Wrong claims: describing things that are not in the video.
  • No links at all: missing the chance to send viewers to another of your videos.

Improve old videos

You can edit descriptions at any time. Open YouTube Studio, find a video that gets some impressions but low views, and rewrite the first two lines and add chapters. Wait a couple of weeks, then compare the numbers. Small description updates will not rescue a weak video, but they are free and low risk.
