YouTube Hashtags: How Many to Use and Where to Put Them

Published November 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Hashtags are clickable words that start with the # sign. When a viewer taps one, YouTube shows other videos that use the same hashtag. Used well, they help people who browse a topic find your video. Used badly, they can hurt you. This guide explains how hashtags work, how many to use, where to place them and how to choose them honestly.

What a hashtag does on YouTube

A hashtag turns a word into a link. If you add #budgettravel to your video, a viewer who taps that hashtag lands on a page of videos that use it. This gives your video one more way to be discovered, mostly by people who are browsing a topic and not searching for a specific question.

Hashtags are not a shortcut to views. They are a small label that tells viewers and YouTube what the video is about. Your title, thumbnail and first seconds matter far more.

Hashtags vs tags

Tags and hashtags are different things, and you can use both:

  • Tags are hidden keywords in the upload settings. Viewers do not see them. They play a small role, mainly for spelling variations. See our YouTube tags guide.
  • Hashtags are visible. They sit in the description or in the title, and viewers can tap them.

Where to put hashtags

  • In the description: add them at the end, or at the start. The first three hashtags from the description appear above your video title.
  • In the title: possible, but one at most is cleaner. A hashtag in the title takes space away from words that matter more, and if you put hashtags in the title, YouTube shows only those and not the ones from the description.

For most videos, the description is the better place. It keeps the title clean and readable.

How many hashtags to use

YouTube states that if a video has more than 15 hashtags, it ignores all of them for that video. That is the upper limit, not a target. A safe approach is three to five relevant hashtags. Since only the first three show above the title, put the most important ones first.

How to choose your hashtags

  1. One broad topic hashtag. For a travel video: #travel.
  2. One or two specific hashtags. For example #jaisalmer or #budgettravel.
  3. One hashtag for your channel or series (optional). This lets viewers find your other videos in the same series.

Write them as one word with no spaces. If a hashtag has more than one word, you can capitalise each word for readability, such as #BudgetTravel. It makes no difference to how YouTube reads it.

A simple example

This example is illustrative, not real channel data. Imagine a video about a three-day budget trip to Jaisalmer.

HashtagWhy it fits
#travelBroad topic, matches the video
#jaisalmerThe real place shown in the video
#budgettravelMatches the actual focus of the video
#RajasthanTravelDiariesA series name for the creator's other trip videos

Every hashtag here describes what is actually in the video. That is the rule to follow.

Hashtags for Shorts

For YouTube Shorts, hashtags in the title or description help place the video with similar content. The same rules apply: keep them short, few and relevant. Our guide on growing with YouTube Shorts covers more about this format.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Trending hashtags that do not match the video. This can be treated as misleading metadata.
  • Adding spaces. A hashtag is one word: #youtubetips, not #youtube tips.
  • Adding too many. Going over 15 means all are ignored. Even ten is usually too many.
  • Using the same hashtags on every video. Each video should have hashtags that fit its own topic.
  • Special characters. Hashtags can include letters and numbers, but symbols and punctuation can break them.

A quick checklist before you publish

  • Three to five hashtags, never more than 15.
  • The most important ones come first.
  • Every hashtag matches what is in the video.
  • No spaces and no unrelated trending words.
  • Hashtags are in the description unless you have a reason to use the title.

Frequently asked questions

Do hashtags help my video rank higher?

They help viewers browsing a topic find your video, but there is no guarantee of a higher ranking. Good titles, thumbnails and viewer reaction matter more.

Should I put hashtags in the title or the description?

The description is usually better. It keeps your title clean. If you do use the title, keep it to one hashtag.

Can I use the same hashtags as a bigger channel?

Only if they honestly describe your own video. Copying hashtags that do not fit can be treated as misleading.

Are hashtags and tags the same thing?

No. Tags are hidden keywords in the upload settings. Hashtags are visible and clickable.

Can I edit hashtags after I publish?

Yes. You can change the description or title at any time in YouTube Studio.

Final thoughts

Hashtags are a small tool. Pick a few that honestly describe your video, put the most important first and move on to the parts that matter more, such as the title and the opening seconds.
