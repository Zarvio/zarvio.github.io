How to Find the Best Day and Time to Upload on YouTube

Updated November 21, 2026 · 9 min read

Many articles claim there is one perfect day and hour to upload. The truth is that it depends on your audience. A channel about exam preparation and a channel about weekend travel have very different viewers. The good news is that you can find your own best time with free data. This guide shows how, step by step, including how to convert time zones and how to read the results of a test fairly.

The short version: check when your viewers are online in YouTube Studio, publish one to two hours before that peak, keep a steady schedule, and test two options for about a month each. Judge the result by average and median views in the first 48 hours, not by one lucky video.

Why there is no universal best time

Your viewers live in certain countries, follow certain routines and watch at certain hours. Students may watch after school, office workers during a lunch break, and travel fans on weekends. A time that works for a gaming channel in the USA may not work for a cooking channel in India. Any list that claims one best time for everyone is a guess, so use your own data instead.

Step 1: Check when your viewers are online

In YouTube Studio, open Analytics, go to the Audience tab and look for the "When your viewers are on YouTube" chart. It shows the days and hours with the most viewers. This is your starting point. If you are a new channel and the chart is empty or very small, wait until you have more views, or use the general habits of your target audience as a temporary guide.

While you are in the Audience tab, also open the Geography section. It tells you which countries your viewers are in, which decides the time zone you should plan around.

Step 2: Convert the time zone correctly

Studio shows the chart in your own time zone, but if a large part of your audience lives elsewhere, you must convert. The table below shows what 19:00 (7 PM) in India looks like in a few other places. These are standard-time values, and they can shift by an hour in places that use daylight saving time.

If most of your viewers are in India, schedule in Indian time. If you have a large audience abroad, pick a time that is not terrible for both groups, or favour the larger group.

Step 3: Upload a little before the peak

YouTube needs a short time to process and begin showing a new video. Publishing one to two hours before your audience's busiest period is a common approach. If you schedule the video in YouTube Studio, you can prepare everything in advance and publish at the exact time you choose.

Step 4: Compare your own videos

Look at your last ten to twenty videos. Note the day each was published and the views it got in the first 48 hours. If videos posted on one weekday regularly do better, that day is worth trying more often. Keep in mind that topic and thumbnail also change the numbers, so look for a repeated pattern, not a single result. The Channel Planner in Trenndly makes this comparison for any public channel, using the weekday of its recent uploads.

Step 5: Be consistent

A steady schedule matters more than a perfect hour. If your viewers know you post every Saturday, they start to come back on that day. Choose a schedule you can keep for months, even if it means posting less often.

Step 6: Run a one-month test

  1. Pick two different days or times, for example Saturday morning and Wednesday evening.
  2. Use the first for about a month, then the second for about a month. Aim for at least four videos in each period.
  3. Write down the views each video got in its first 48 hours.
  4. Change only the upload time. Keep your topics and style as similar as you can.
  5. Compare the results using the method below, keep the better option and then test something new.

How to read your test results fairly

One viral video can make an average look better than it really is. That is why it helps to calculate both the average (add the numbers and divide by the count) and the median (the middle value when you sort the numbers). Here is a worked example with made-up numbers, to show the method only. It is not real channel data.

Month 1: Saturday 10:00120, 90, 150, 140125130
Month 2: Wednesday 19:00100, 110, 95, 115105105

In this example, Month 1 is ahead on both the average (125 against 105) and the median (130 against 105), so Saturday morning looks like the better choice. If the average were higher but the median lower, that would suggest one unusually good video is distorting the result, and you should keep testing. If the numbers are close, upload time is not your main problem, and your time is better spent on titles, thumbnails and hooks.

Common mistakes

  • Testing several things at once. If you change the time, the topic and the thumbnail together, you cannot tell what caused the difference.
  • Judging from one video. A single result can come from the topic or a lucky share.
  • Ignoring the time zone. A "good" time in your city may be the middle of the night for your viewers.
  • Changing the schedule too often. Viewers cannot form a habit if your day keeps moving.
  • Posting late or skipping weeks to stay on a "perfect" time slot.

Things to remember

  • Shorts and long videos behave differently. Shorts are shown through a feed all day, so upload time matters less for them. See our Shorts guide.
  • Quality matters more than timing. A great video posted at an odd hour will beat a weak video posted at the "perfect" hour.
  • Small channels have little data. Be patient and keep testing.
  • Festivals, exams and holidays change when people watch, so note them in your test log.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best day to upload on YouTube?

There is no single best day for everyone. Check your own audience chart in YouTube Studio and test two options for a month each.

Does upload time affect ranking?

Not directly in a way you can control. Timing mostly affects how many people see the video in the first hours, which is why publishing before your audience's peak can help.

Should I schedule videos or publish instantly?

Scheduling is better, because you can choose the exact time and keep a steady rhythm.

How often should I upload?

As often as you can keep up with good quality. One strong video a week is better than five rushed ones.

How do I know if my test worked?

Compare the average and median views in the first 48 hours across the two periods. If the difference is small, timing is not your biggest lever. Read our guide on audience retention for what to check next.

Final thoughts

Find when your viewers are online, convert the time zone correctly, publish a little before the peak, stay consistent and test one change at a time. Your own data will always beat a generic list.
