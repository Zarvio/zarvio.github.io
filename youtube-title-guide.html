How to Write a YouTube Title That Gets Clicks Honestly

Published November 18, 2026 · 7 min read

Your title and thumbnail work together as a promise to the viewer. A good title makes the right person curious, and your video then keeps that promise. This guide shows how to write titles that are clear, interesting and honest, with examples you can adapt to your own topic.

What a title has to do

A title has two jobs. First, it helps YouTube and viewers understand what the video is about. Second, it gives the right viewer a reason to click. A title that only does the first job is dull. A title that only does the second job is clickbait. The best titles do both, and the video then delivers what was promised.

Keep the length in mind

YouTube allows titles up to 100 characters, but search results and phones often cut a title after roughly 60 to 70 characters. Put the most important words at the start so nothing important is hidden. A longer title is fine if the first half works on its own.

Put the keyword early

Think about what a viewer would type to find your video, and use that phrase near the beginning. A viewer should understand the topic in one glance. Write for a person first. A title that reads like a list of search terms feels spammy and gets fewer clicks.

Add one reason to click

A keyword alone is often dull. Add one element that creates interest:

  • A specific result: "Jaisalmer in 3 Days: Full Budget Plan and Real Costs".
  • A number: "7 Mistakes New Photographers Make".
  • A question the viewer already has: "Is a Used Laptop Worth It in 2026?"
  • A clear benefit: "Edit Videos Faster With 5 Simple Shortcuts".
  • A time or cost limit: "A Healthy Dinner in 15 Minutes".

Pick one, not all five. A title that tries to do everything becomes long and confusing.

Write the title and thumbnail together

The thumbnail and title should not repeat each other. If the title says "I Cooked Pasta for 30 Days", the thumbnail can show "Day 30". Each adds something new. Read our guide on designing a thumbnail to see how the two fit together.

Do not mislead

If the title promises something the video does not deliver, viewers leave early. A low watch time tells YouTube that people were not satisfied. Clickbait can give a small spike and then hurt your channel. YouTube's policies also restrict misleading titles and thumbnails. Make the title exciting, but always true.

Common title mistakes

  • Putting the main keyword at the very end, where it may be cut off.
  • ALL CAPS and too many exclamation marks.
  • Vague titles such as "Vlog" or "Update" with no topic.
  • Stuffing several keywords separated by dashes or pipes.
  • Copying a competitor's title word for word. Use the pattern, not the wording. Our guide on competitor research explains how.

Test and learn

  1. Write five different titles for each video before choosing one. Pick the clearest, not the loudest.
  2. After a few days, open YouTube Studio and check impressions click-through rate and average view duration.
  3. If people see your video but do not click, the title or thumbnail needs work.
  4. If they click but leave quickly, the opening of the video needs work. See our guide on writing a strong hook.
  5. You can edit a title at any time, so improve older videos that get impressions but few clicks.

Checklist

  • Main keyword in the first half.
  • Around 60 characters or less when possible.
  • One clear reason to click.
  • No misleading words and no ALL CAPS.
  • The video really delivers what the title says.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a YouTube title be?

The limit is 100 characters, but aim for about 60 so it is not cut off in search and on phones.

Should I always include a number?

No. Numbers work well for lists and results, but a clear question or benefit can be just as good. Use what fits your video.

Can I change a title after publishing?

Yes. You can edit titles in YouTube Studio at any time. Change one thing at a time so you can see the effect.

Do titles need hashtags?

Not usually. Hashtags work better in the description. See our hashtags guide.

Can a great title guarantee more views?

No. It helps the right viewers decide to click, but views also depend on your topic, thumbnail and how viewers react to the video.

Final thoughts

A good title is clear, specific and true. Put the topic first, add one reason to click and make sure the video keeps the promise.
