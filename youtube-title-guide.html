How to Write a YouTube Title That Gets Clicks Honestly

Updated November 21, 2026 · 9 min read

A title is a promise made in about ten words. If it is vague, nobody clicks. If it over-promises, people click and leave. This guide gives you a simple formula, shows exactly where phones cut your title, walks through step-by-step rewrites, and ends with a short audit you can run on any title in two minutes.

The short version: a good title has three parts: Topic (what the video is about), Angle (why this video, for whom) and Detail (a number, price, time or result). Put the topic first, keep it near 60 characters, and make sure the video really delivers what the title says.

What a title actually does

A title does two jobs at once. It tells YouTube and the viewer what the video is about, and it gives the right viewer a reason to click now. A title that does only the first job reads like a file name. A title that does only the second job is clickbait. The thumbnail sits next to the title, so the two work as one package: the thumbnail catches the eye, and the title explains and convinces.

The 3-part title formula

Most strong titles can be broken into three parts. You do not need all three every time, but you should always have the first one.

  1. Topic: the words a viewer would type to find this video, such as "Jaisalmer trip" or "paneer butter masala".
  2. Angle: who it is for or what makes it different: "on a budget", "for beginners", "honest checklist".
  3. Detail: one concrete element: a number, a price, a time limit or a result.

Where phones cut your title

YouTube allows up to 100 characters, but that does not mean all 100 are shown. In search results, suggested videos and on phones, long titles are often cut off, usually somewhere around 60 to 70 characters, and the exact point changes with the device and screen. Look at this 99-character title:

How to Edit YouTube Videos on Your Phone for Free With Apps, Tips and Tricks for Complete Beginners

A viewer may see only this much: "How to Edit YouTube Videos on Your Phone for Free With Apps," and the rest is hidden. The most useful words ("Complete Beginners") are in the hidden part. Compare that with a title that works even when it is cut: "Edit Videos on Your Phone for Free (Beginner Guide)", which is 51 characters and shows everything.

A good habit: write the title, then read only the first 60 characters. If the first 60 characters still make sense and give a reason to click, the title is safe.

A step-by-step rewrite

Let us start with a vague title and improve it one step at a time. The topic is editing videos on a phone.

VersionTitleCharactersWhat we changed
1Editing Video13Starting point: no topic detail, no audience
2Edit Videos on Your Phone25Named the device, so the topic is now clear
3Edit Videos on Your Phone for Free (Beginner Guide)51Added price and audience
4Phone Video Editing: 3 Free Apps32Added a number; shorter and easier to scan

Version 3 and version 4 are both good. Which is better depends on the video. If the video is a step-by-step tutorial, version 3 sets the right expectation. If the video compares three apps, version 4 is more honest. The best title is the one that matches what is actually in the video.

Titles in Hindi, English or Hinglish

Many Indian viewers search in Hindi, English or a mix of both written in English letters. Which one should you use? Write the title in the language your viewers use when they search, and the language you speak in the video. A Hinglish title such as "Phone Se Video Edit Kaise Kare: Free Apps, Step by Step" (55 characters) matches a viewer who types their question that way.

A simple way to check: type the start of your topic into the YouTube search box in each language and see which suggestions appear. The suggestions show what real people type. Do not mix languages randomly inside one title just to add keywords. Choose one style and keep it consistent across your channel.

Honest does not mean boring

YouTube has policies against misleading titles and thumbnails, and viewers also leave quickly when a video does not match its title. Exciting and honest can go together. Here is a rule of thumb: if someone watched the first minute, would they feel the title was fair? If not, change the title or the video.

  • Do not promise a result the video cannot show ("Earn Rs 1 Lakh a Month" with no proof).
  • Do not use ALL CAPS or rows of exclamation marks to look urgent.
  • Do not name a famous person or event that is not really in the video.
  • Do not copy a competitor's title word for word. Learn the pattern, then write your own. Our guide on competitor research shows how.

Write the title and thumbnail together

The title and thumbnail should add to each other, not repeat each other. If the title says "Edit Videos on Your Phone for Free", the thumbnail can show a before-and-after frame with two or three words. Each part gives the viewer something new. Our thumbnail guide covers sizes and layout.

A 2-minute title audit

Before you publish, give your title one point for every "yes":

  1. Is the main topic in the first 60 characters?
  2. Can someone who has never seen your channel understand it?
  3. Does it contain one concrete detail (a number, price, time or result)?
  4. Would the first minute of the video make this title feel fair?
  5. Is it free of ALL CAPS, stacked punctuation and keyword stuffing?

5 points: publish it. 3 or 4 points: fix the missing items. 2 points or fewer: rewrite from the formula above.

How to judge AI title suggestions

Trenndly's Analyze tool suggests five title ideas after you upload a video. Treat them as a first draft, in the same way you would treat a suggestion from a friend. Run each one through the audit above, then ask four extra questions:

  • Is every claim in the title really in my video?
  • Does it sound like how I speak and write?
  • Is it too similar to a very popular video that already exists?
  • Can I make it shorter without losing the topic?

Often the best final title is a mix: the topic from one suggestion and the detail from another. For more on the other parts of your upload, see our guides on tags, descriptions and hashtags.

How to see if a new title works

  1. Open YouTube Studio and look at impressions click-through rate and average view duration for the video.
  2. Compare with your own earlier videos, not with a number from the internet. Normal click-through rates differ a lot between topics and channels.
  3. If people see the video but do not click, work on the title and the thumbnail.
  4. If they click but leave quickly, the title may promise more than the video gives, or the start of the video is weak. Read our hook guide.
  5. You can edit a title at any time. Change one thing at a time, wait a few days and then compare, so you know what caused the difference.

The timing of your upload can also affect early views. See when to upload a video on YouTube.

Checklist

  • Topic in the first half, and clear in the first 60 characters.
  • One reason to click: a number, price, time or result.
  • No misleading words, no ALL CAPS.
  • Language matches your viewers and your video.
  • The video delivers what the title promises.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a YouTube title be?

The limit is 100 characters, but many titles are cut off after roughly 60 to 70. Aim for about 60 and keep the topic at the start.

Should I always include a number?

No. Numbers suit lists and results. A clear question or a plain benefit can work just as well. Use what fits your video.

Should the title be in Hindi or English?

Use the language your viewers search in and the language you speak in the video. Type your topic in the YouTube search box to see which suggestions appear.

Can I change a title after publishing?

Yes, at any time in YouTube Studio. Change one thing at a time so you can see the effect.

Do titles need hashtags?

Usually not. Hashtags fit better in the description. See our hashtags guide.

Can a great title guarantee more views?

No. It helps the right viewers decide to click, but views also depend on your topic, thumbnail and how viewers react to the video.

Final thoughts

Start with the topic, add one concrete detail, keep it short enough to survive the cut, and make sure the video keeps the promise. Run every title through the audit before you publish.
