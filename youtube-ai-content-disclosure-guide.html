How to Disclose AI-Generated Content on YouTube

Published November 18, 2026 · 6 min read

More creators now use AI tools to make video, voices and images. YouTube allows this, but it asks creators to be open when the result could be mistaken for real. This guide explains the idea in plain language, how to turn on the disclosure and how to keep your channel safe. Rules change over time, so always confirm the latest details on YouTube's official Help pages.

The basic rule

YouTube asks you to tell viewers when your video contains realistic content that was meaningfully altered or generated with AI, in a way that someone could believe is real. The goal is to stop viewers from being misled about what actually happened. It is not meant to punish people for using AI.

When you should disclose

Examples where a disclosure is normally expected:

  • A realistic-looking person saying or doing something they never said or did.
  • A cloned or synthetic voice that sounds like a real person.
  • Footage of a real place or event that has been altered, for example making a real building appear damaged or changing what happened at a real event.
  • A realistic scene that never happened, such as a lifelike video of a made-up disaster or news event.

The closer the video looks to a real recording, and the more serious the topic, the more important it is to disclose. Topics like health, news, elections and finance need extra care.

When you usually do not need to disclose

  • Clearly unrealistic content, such as animation, fantasy scenes or cartoons.
  • Using AI to help with your work, such as writing a script, brainstorming ideas, generating captions or improving audio quality.
  • Minor edits like color correction, beauty filters, blur or background changes that do not change the meaning of the video.

If you are not sure, the safer choice is to disclose. A label is much less harmful to a channel than being accused of deceiving viewers.

How to add the disclosure

  1. Open YouTube Studio and start an upload, or open an existing video.
  2. In the video details, scroll to the section about altered or synthetic content.
  3. Choose "Yes" if your video contains realistic altered or synthetic content.
  4. Save and publish.

YouTube then shows a label in the video description. For more sensitive topics, the label may appear more prominently on the player. You can also add a short note yourself in the description, such as "Scenes in this video were generated with AI."

Be honest about the visible watermark and hidden marks

Many AI video tools add a visible watermark and also embed invisible marks, such as SynthID, inside the video. Removing a visible watermark from a video that you made yourself is a personal editing choice, and it does not remove those hidden signals or your duty to disclose realistic AI content. Never use watermark removal on videos that belong to someone else, and never use it to pass off AI content as real footage.

Mass-produced and repetitive content

Disclosure is only one part of the picture. YouTube's monetization rules also warn against mass-produced, repetitive or inauthentic content. Channels that upload large numbers of near-identical videos made from templates, or that simply read out reused text over stock clips, may not be accepted into the YouTube Partner Program or may lose monetization.

To stay on the right side:

  • Add your own value: your voice, your opinion, your research, your editing or your story.
  • Make each video clearly different, not a copy with small swaps.
  • Check facts before you publish, especially if AI helped to write the script.
  • Do not clone real people's faces or voices without permission.

Copyright and other people

AI tools do not remove copyright. Do not generate content that copies a specific copyrighted character, brand, song or a real person's likeness in a way that is harmful or deceptive. If someone's face or voice is cloned without permission, YouTube also has a privacy process that allows people to request removal.

A quick checklist before you upload

  • Could a viewer reasonably think this video shows something real that did not happen?
  • Have I turned on the altered or synthetic content setting if needed?
  • Have I checked the facts and the sources?
  • Do I have the rights to every image, clip and sound?
  • Is the video clearly my own work and not a copy of a template?

Final thoughts

Trust is the most valuable thing a channel has. Clear disclosure costs you almost nothing and protects that trust. If you create AI videos, read our guide on writing better AI video prompts to make clips that are original, and follow YouTube's official policy pages for the latest rules.
