How to Write YouTube Tags That Actually Help

Written by the Trenndly Team · 8 min read · Updated November 2026

Tags are keywords you add in the "Show more" section when you upload a video. Viewers do not see them on the watch page, so many creators wonder whether tags matter at all. This guide gives an honest answer, a simple method for writing them, a full worked example, and answers to the questions creators ask most.

What tags really do

YouTube has said that tags play only a minor role in how your video is found. Their main use is to help the system understand your topic, especially when people commonly misspell a word in your subject or when a word has more than one meaning. Your title, thumbnail, description, spoken words and the way viewers react to the video matter far more.

So treat tags as a small but useful finishing step, not a growth trick. They take about two minutes, they cost nothing, and done honestly they can only help. Done dishonestly, they can hurt you, which we cover below.

Tags, hashtags and keywords: what is the difference?

These three get mixed up all the time, so here is the short version.

  • Tags are hidden. They live in the upload settings and are never shown to viewers.
  • Hashtags are visible. You type them with a # in your title or description, and the first few appear above your title. Our hashtags guide explains how to use them.
  • Keywords are the words and phrases people type into search. Good tags, a good title and a good description are all built from the same keywords.

Step by step: how to choose good tags

  1. Start with your main keyword. If your video is about making masala chai, your first tag should be exactly that phrase: "masala chai recipe". The first tag carries the most weight for describing your topic.
  2. Add close variations. Think about how different people would search: "how to make masala tea", "indian chai at home". These are the same idea in different words.
  3. Use some longer phrases. A phrase of three or more words describes your video more precisely than a single broad word like "tea". Broad words are shared by millions of videos, so they say very little about yours.
  4. Include misspellings only if they are common. If a lot of people really spell a word the wrong way, one tag with that spelling is fine. Do not invent misspellings.
  5. Add your channel or series name if you make videos in a series. This helps YouTube connect your videos to each other.
  6. Read the list once more as a viewer. For every tag ask: would a person who typed this be happy to land on my video? If not, delete it.

A worked example

Imagine a 6 minute video titled "Easy Masala Chai in 10 Minutes (Indian Home Recipe)". Here is a tag list we might build for it, and the reason behind each choice.

TagWhy it is there
masala chai recipeMain keyword, exactly what the video is about
how to make masala chaiQuestion form that people type into search
indian chai at homeClose variation with the "at home" angle
masala teaCommon alternative word for the same drink
10 minute chaiMatches the promise in the title
easy indian tea recipeLonger phrase for beginners
chai spicesTopic covered in the video
ginger cardamom teaIngredients shown in the video
indian street style chaiStyle of the recipe
home cookingBroad category, kept as only one broad tag

This list has ten tags, a mix of short and long phrases, and about 230 characters in total. Every tag is something the video really delivers. Notice what is missing: no famous chef names, no unrelated trending topics, and no repeated copies of the same word.

How many tags should you use?

YouTube allows up to 500 characters in total. You do not need to fill all of it. Eight to fifteen relevant tags are usually enough. A few accurate tags are better than thirty loosely related ones, because every extra weak tag makes your topic a little blurrier.

Where to find tag ideas

  • YouTube search autocomplete. Type your topic in the search box and note the suggestions. They show what people actually search.
  • Similar videos that perform well. Public tag viewers, including the Tag Finder in Trenndly, show the tags other videos use. Use them for inspiration, not for copying. Our guide on competitor research explains how to do this honestly.
  • Your own comments. Viewers often use the exact words they search with.
  • Your spoken words. If you say a phrase several times in the video, it is probably a good tag.

Where to add tags in YouTube Studio

  1. Open YouTube Studio and click Content in the left menu.
  2. Click the title of your video to open its details page.
  3. Scroll down and click Show more.
  4. Find the Tags box, paste your tags separated by commas, and press Save.

On the mobile YouTube Studio app, add tags one at a time. Tap a tag to copy it, paste it, and repeat. You can change tags on a published video at any time.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Misleading tags. Adding a popular name or topic that has nothing to do with your video breaks YouTube's spam and deceptive practices policy and can hurt your channel. A creator who tags every video with the name of a trending celebrity may get a short burst of clicks, but viewers leave quickly and the video can be removed or limited.
  • Copying everything from a competitor. Their video may be about something slightly different. Keep only the tags that truly describe yours.
  • Tag stuffing. Repeating the same word in many forms adds nothing.
  • Using only broad tags. "Video", "funny" and "tutorial" tell YouTube almost nothing.
  • Expecting tags to fix a weak video. If the title, thumbnail and first ten seconds do not make people click and stay, tags will not rescue it. Work on those first, starting with our guides on titles and hooks.

Quick checklist

  • First tag is your main keyword.
  • Every tag honestly matches the video.
  • Mix of short and long phrases.
  • Eight to fifteen tags, under 500 characters in total.
  • No celebrity names or trending topics unless the video is really about them.

Frequently asked questions

Do tags still matter in 2026?

Yes, but only a little. They help YouTube understand your topic and handle misspellings. Your title, thumbnail, description and viewer behavior matter much more.

Do YouTube Shorts need tags?

You can add them, but they matter even less on Shorts. For Shorts, a clear hook and a good first second do most of the work. See our Shorts growth guide.

Can I change tags after I publish?

Yes. Open the video in YouTube Studio, go to Show more, edit the tags and save. There is no penalty for changing them.

Should I use the same tags on every video?

Only your channel or series name should repeat. The rest should describe each video individually.

Is it okay to use a competitor's tags?

You can look at them for ideas, but only keep the ones that honestly match your own video. Copying a whole list is a bad habit and often does not fit your content.

How are tags different from hashtags?

Tags are hidden and live in the upload settings. Hashtags are visible and appear in your title or description. Read the hashtags guide for details.

Tags will not make a weak video go viral, but accurate tags help YouTube understand your content, and that is worth two minutes at upload time.
