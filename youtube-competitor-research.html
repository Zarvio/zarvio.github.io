How to Do Competitor Research on YouTube Without Copying

Trenndly Blog · 5 min read · Updated November 2026

Studying other channels in your niche is one of the fastest ways to learn what viewers want. The goal is not to copy, it is to understand patterns and find gaps you can fill with your own voice.

Step 1: Pick the right channels

Choose three to five channels in your niche. Include one or two that are about your size, because their strategy is closer to what you can do now, and one or two bigger channels to learn from.

Step 2: Look at their best videos

On a channel page, sort the videos by "Popular". Write down the topics of the top ten. If similar topics appear again and again, viewers clearly want them.

Step 3: Study titles and thumbnails

Notice patterns in how their top videos are named. Do they use numbers, questions or specific results? Notice what the thumbnail shows. Use the idea behind a pattern, not the exact words.

Step 4: Compare tags

Public tags of a video show how a creator describes it. Comparing a competitor's tags with yours can reveal related phrases you missed. Add only the tags that honestly match your video.

Step 5: Check upload habits

How often do they post, and on which days? A consistent schedule is often a hidden reason for steady growth.

Step 6: Find the gaps

Read the comments on their popular videos. Questions that are unanswered, requests for a simpler explanation or complaints about quality are ideas for your next videos.

Keep it ethical

  • Never reuse someone's video, script or thumbnail.
  • Use research for ideas, then add your own experience and style.
  • Credit sources when you use someone else's information.

Simple weekly routine

Spend thirty minutes a week reviewing your competitors' newest uploads and note one idea to test. Over a few months this habit gives you a clear view of your niche.
