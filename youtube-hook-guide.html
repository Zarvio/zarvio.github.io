How to Write a Strong Video Hook: Your First 10 Seconds

Trenndly Blog · 6 min read · Updated November 2026

A hook is what happens in the first few seconds of your video. Viewers decide very quickly whether to stay or leave, so a strong hook protects all the effort you put into the rest of the video.

What a good hook does

A good hook does three things: it tells the viewer the video is for them, it shows what they will get, and it gives them a reason to keep watching.

Five hook styles that work

  1. Show the result first. Start with the finished dish, the final edit or the big moment, then say "here is how I did it".
  2. Name the problem. "Your videos get views but nobody subscribes? Here is why."
  3. Open a loop. Mention something you will reveal later: "By the end, you will see the one setting that doubled my watch time."
  4. Make a clear promise. "In 60 seconds I will show you how to fix shaky footage."
  5. Start in the middle of the action. Skip the slow lead-in and jump into the most interesting moment.

Example

Weak: "Hi guys, welcome back to my channel, today we are going to talk about editing." Better: "This one cut made my video 30 seconds shorter and kept more people watching. Let me show you."

Common hook mistakes

  • A long logo animation or intro before any value.
  • Saying hello and asking people to subscribe before giving a reason to stay.
  • Silent or music-only openings when your video needs a voice to explain.
  • Promising something in the hook that the video never delivers.

Do not forget silent viewers

Many people watch with the sound off. Adding a short line of on-screen text in the first seconds helps them understand what the video is about.

How to check your hook

Open YouTube Studio, go to Analytics, and look at the audience retention graph of a video. A steep drop in the first 15 seconds means the opening is not holding people. Compare your videos and note which openings keep more viewers, then repeat what works.

Quick checklist

  • Value or result shown in the first 5 seconds.
  • No long intro.
  • Clear promise that the video keeps.
  • On-screen text for viewers without sound.
